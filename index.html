Why we chosethis challenge

We chose Apollo 13 because it is the best example of a real NASA mission where one small failure changed everything.

STEP 1 · THE MISSION

Apollo 13 - A Mission To The Moon

NASA PHOTO

Apollo 13 launched on April 11, 1970. The goal was to land on the Moon for the third time. The crew was Jim Lovell, Jack Swigert, and Fred Haise.

STEP 2 · THE PROBLEM

The Explosion - April 13, 1970

NASA PHOTO⚠ CRITICAL FAILURE - O2 TANK 2

About 56 hours into the mission (55 hours 55 minutes), about 330,000 km (205,000 miles) from Earth, Oxygen Tank No. 2 in the Service Module exploded. The crew heard a loud bang and saw the oxygen and power dropping fast. They had to abort the Moon landing.

STEP 3 · THE REAL CAUSES - FROM NASA REFERENCES

Why Did It Happen? Real Causes

Damaged Thermostat

The tank heater had thermostat switches that should cut it off at 80°F. During an eight-hour ground test in March 1970 they welded shut, and the wiring inside the tank reached about 1000°F in places.

Source: NASA NSSDCA and NASA History Office (see Sources).
Damaged Insulation

The high heat damaged the Teflon insulation on the wires inside the tank. When the fan was turned on in space, it created a short circuit and fire.

Source: NASA History Office (see Sources).
Hidden Damage

In 1968 the tank was dropped about 2 inches, slightly jarring a fill line inside it. The damage was not known, and the tank was later installed in Apollo 13. The loose line made the tank hard to empty on the ground, which led to the long heating.

Source: NASA History Office and NASA NSSDCA (see Sources).

The problemwe solve

Four steps, from a blank page to a launch.

CHAPTER 1 OF 4

The budget problem

Every mission has a fixed budget and a science goal. Spend too much and the mission is cancelled. Spend too little and we cannot reach the goal.

CHAPTER 2 OF 4

The weight problem

A rocket can only lift so much. Every instrument, tank and solar panel adds mass, and more mass needs more fuel. Atlas lifted about 1.4 tonnes. Saturn V lifted about 140.

CHAPTER 3 OF 4

The parts problem

We choose the launch vehicle, engine, staging, power and instruments. Every part trades one thing for another: power for mass, science for cost.

CHAPTER 4 OF 4

Our solution

Start with the goal, pick the lightest rocket that can lift the load, add only parts that raise the science score, and keep a reserve.

  1. 1Read the budget and the science goal.
  2. 2Pick a launch vehicle that can lift the mass.
  3. 3Choose the engine and enough fuel.
  4. 4Add power and instruments that earn points.
  5. 5Watch the Mass and Cost bars and run Ship Check.
  6. 6Launch, then adapt when events happen.
